概率模型的基本术语
The Basic Vocabulary of Probability Models

Page 1

1 Learning Goals

  1. Know the definitions of sample space, event and probability function.
  2. Be able to organize a scenario with randomness into an experiment and sample space.
  3. Be able to make basic computations using a probability function.

2.1 Probability cast list

  • Experiment: a repeatable procedure with well-defined possible outcomes.
  • Sample space: the set of all possible outcomes. We usually denote the sample space by Ω, sometimes by S.
  • Event: a subset of the sample space.
  • Probability function: a function giving the probability for each outcome.

Later in the course we will learn about

  • Probability density: a continuous distribution of probabilities.
  • Random variable: a random numerical outcome.

学习目标要求掌握 experiment 试验、sample space 样本空间、event 事件和 probability function 概率函数. 面对随机场景时, 先明确做什么试验、可能结果有哪些, 再给每个结果分配概率.

样本空间 Ω 是全部分子结果的集合; 事件不是另一种对象, 而是 Ω 的子集. 本讲先讨论可列的离散结果, 连续情形要用 probability density 概率密度, random variable 随机变量则把结果映射成数值.

2.2 Simple examples

Example 1. Toss a fair coin.

Experiment: toss the coin, report if it lands heads or tails.

Sample space: Ω={H,T}.

Probability function: P(H)=0.5, P(T)=0.5.

Example 2. Toss a fair coin 3 times.

Experiment: toss the coin 3 times, list the results.

Sample space:

Ω={HHH,HHT,HTH,HTT,THH,THT,TTH,TTT}.

Probability function: Each outcome is equally likely with probability 1/8.

For small sample spaces we can put the set of outcomes and probabilities into a probability table.

OutcomesHHHHHTHTHHTTTHHTHTTTHTTT
Probability1/81/81/81/81/81/81/81/8

单次抛硬币只有 H,T 两个结果, 各为 0.5. 抛 3 次时, 一个结果是完整的有序序列, 共有 23=8 个, 所以公平硬币的每个序列概率为 1/8.

概率表把结果和概率一一对应. 对后面更复杂的试验, 先列出这张表往往能避免把“序列”与“正面次数”混为一谈.

Example 3. Measure the mass of a proton

Experiment: follow some defined procedure to measure the mass and report the result.

Sample space: Ω=[0,∞), i.e. in principle we can get any positive value.

Probability function: since there is a continuum of possible outcomes there is no probability function. Instead we need to use a probability density, which we will learn about later in the course.

Example 4. Taxis (An infinite discrete sample space)

Experiment: count the number of taxis that pass 77 Mass. Ave during an 18.05 class.

Sample space: Ω={0,1,2,3,4,…}.

This is often modeled with the following probability function known as the Poisson distribution. (Do not worry about mastering the Poisson distribution just yet):

P(k)=e−λk!λk​,

where λ is the average number of taxis. We can put this in a table:

Outcome0123…k…
Probabilitye−λe−λλe−λλ2/2e−λλ3/3!…e−λλk/k!…

Question: Accepting that this is a valid probability function, what is ∑k=0∞​e−λλk/k!?

Solution: This is the total probability of all possible outcomes, so the sum equals 1.

(Note, this also follows from the Taylor series eλ=∑n=0∞​λn/n!.)

质子质量的测量结果在连续区间内, 不能用本讲的离散概率函数逐点分配概率, 而要用密度对区间积分. “没有概率函数”指没有这里所定义的离散概率质量函数, 不是没有概率模型.

出租车数是非负整数, 样本空间可列但无限. Poisson distribution 泊松分布给 k 辆车出现的概率 e−λλk/k!, 其中 λ 是平均计数.

所有概率相加为 1: ∑k=0∞​e−λλk/k!=e−λeλ=1. 这里既可以用“总概率为 1”解释, 也可以用 eλ 的泰勒级数验证.

Events.

An event is a collection of outcomes, i.e. an event is a subset of the sample space Ω. This sounds odd, but it actually corresponds to the common meaning of the word.

Example 6. Using the setup in Example ?? we would describe the event that you get exactly two heads in words by E= ‘exactly 2 heads’. Written as a subset this becomes

E={HHT,HTH,THH}.

You should get comfortable moving between describing events in words and as subsets of the sample space.

The probability of an event E is computed by adding up the probabilities of all of the outcomes in E. In this example each outcome has probability 1/8, so we have P(E)=3/8.

2.3 Definition of a discrete sample space

Definition. A discrete sample space is one that is listable, it can be either finite or infinite.

Examples. {H,T}, {1,2,3}, {1,2,3,4,…}, {2,3,5,7,11,13,17,…} are all discrete sets. The first two are finite and the last two are infinite.

Example. The interval 0≤x≤1 is not discrete, rather it is continuous. We will deal with continuous sample spaces in a few days.

事件就是样本空间的子集. “恰好两次正面”对应 E={HHT,HTH,THH}, 每个序列概率为 1/8, 所以 P(E)=3/8. 把自然语言翻译成集合, 再把集合中结果的概率相加, 是离散概率的基本工作流程.

Discrete sample space 离散样本空间是可以逐个列出的集合, 包括有限集和可数无限集. 区间 [0,1] 中的点无法这样一一列出, 属于连续样本空间.

2.4 The probability function

So far we’ve been using a casual definition of the probability function. Let’s give a more precise one.

Careful definition of the probability function.

For a discrete sample space S a probability function P assigns to each outcome ω a number P(ω) called the probability of ω. P must satisfy two rules:

  • Rule 1. 0≤P(ω)≤1 (probabilities are between 0 and 1).
  • Rule 2. The sum of the probabilities of all possible outcomes is 1 (something must occur)

In symbols, Rule 2 says: if S={ω1​,ω2​,…,ωn​} then P(ω1​)+P(ω2​)+⋯+P(ωn​)=1. Or, using summation notation: ∑j=1n​P(ωj​)=1.

The probability of an event E is the sum of the probabilities of all the outcomes in E. That is,

P(E)=ω∈E∑​P(ω).

Think: Check Rules 1 and 2 on Examples 1 and 2 above.

离散概率函数给每个结果 ω 一个概率 P(ω), 必须满足非负且不超过 1, 以及所有结果的概率和为 1. 事件概率则用 P(E)=∑ω∈E​P(ω) 计算.

例 1 中 0.5+0.5=1, 例 2 中八个 1/8 相加为 1, 都满足两条规则. 对可数无限样本空间, 第二条规则理解为无穷级数之和为 1, 前面的泊松分布就是一例.

Example 7. Flip until heads (A classic example)

Suppose we have a coin with probability p of heads and we have the following scenario.

Experiment: Toss the coin until the first heads. Report the number of tosses.

Sample space: Ω={1,2,3,…}.

Probability function: P(n)=(1−p)n−1p.

Challenge 1: show the sum of all the probabilities equals 1 (hint: geometric series).

Challenge 2: justify the formula for P(n) (we will do this soon).

Stopping problems. The previous toy example is an uncluttered version of a general class of problems called stopping rule problems. A stopping rule is a rule that tells you when to end a certain process. In the toy example above the process was flipping a coin and we stopped after the first heads. A more practical example is a rule for ending a series of medical treatments. Such a rule could depend on how well the treatments are working, how the patient is tolerating them and the probability that the treatments would continue to be effective. One could ask about the probability of stopping within a certain number of treatments or the average number of treatments you should expect before stopping.

第 n 次才第一次出现正面, 要求前 n−1 次都是反面、最后一次是正面. 若各次独立, 概率就是 (1−p)n−1p.

令 q=1−p. 当 0<p≤1 时, ∣q∣<1, 几何级数给出 ∑n=1∞​qn−1p=p/(1−q)=1. 这就是后面几何分布的模型, 也是 stopping rule 停止规则的一例: 一旦出现某个条件就结束过程.

幻灯片错误:Example 7 没有写出 p 的限制, 但“概率和为 1”需要 0<p≤1. 若 p=0, 永远不会出现正面, 所给样本空间没有包含“永不停止”, 而且所有 P(n) 都为 0, 总和不是 1. 上面推导已明确采用 0<p≤1.

3 Some rules of probability

For events A,L and R contained in a sample space Ω.

Rule 1. P(Ac)=1−P(A).

Rule 2. If L and R are disjoint then P(L∪R)=P(L)+P(R).

Rule 3. If L and R are not disjoint, we have the inclusion-exclusion principle:

P(L∪R)=P(L)+P(R)−P(L∩R)

We visualize these rules using Venn diagrams.

We can also justify them logically.

Rule 1: A and Ac split Ω into two non-overlapping regions. Since the total probability P(Ω)=1 this rule says that the probabiity of A and the probability of ’not A’ are complementary, i.e. sum to 1.

Rule 2: L and R split L∪R into two non-overlapping regions. So the probability of L∪R is is split between P(L) and P(R)

Rule 3: In the sum P(L)+P(R) the overlap P(L∩R) gets counted twice. So P(L)+P(R)−P(L∩R) counts everything in the union exactly once.

Think: Rule 2 is a special case of Rule 3.

补集规则来自 A 与 Ac 把全集分成两部分: P(A)+P(Ac)=1. 不相交事件的并集概率可以直接相加; 有重叠时, 交集被加了两次, 所以要减去一次.

Rule 2 是 Rule 3 的特例: 若 L,R 不相交, 则 L∩R=∅, P(L∩R)=0, 容斥公式就退化为 P(L∪R)=P(L)+P(R).

读图:左图的 A 与 Ac 覆盖整个 Ω; 中图两圆没有重叠; 右图重叠区域对应 L∩R, 是容斥中需要减去的部分.

原文笔误:Rule 1 的解释中 probabiity 应为 probability; Rule 2 的解释中 is is split 重复了 is, 应为 is split. 两条公式本身正确.

For the following examples suppose we have an experiment that produces a random integer between 1 and 20. The probabilities are not necessarily uniform, i.e., not necessarily the same for each outcome.

Example 8. If the probability of an even number is 0.6 what is the probability of an odd number?

Solution: Since being odd is complementary to being even, the probability of being odd is 1-0.6 = 0.4.

Let’s redo this example a bit more formally, so you see how it’s done. First, so we can refer to it, let’s name the random integer X. Let’s also name the event ‘X is even’ as A. Then the event ‘X is odd’ is Ac. We are given that P(A)=0.6. Therefore P(Ac)=1−0.6=0.4.

Example 9. Consider the 2 events, A: ‘X is a multiple of 2’; B: ‘X is odd and less than 10’. Suppose P(A)=0.6 and P(B)=0.25.

(i) What is A∩B?
(ii) What is the probability of A∪B?

Solution: (i) Since all numbers in A are even and all numbers in B are odd, these events are disjoint. That is, A∩B=∅.

(ii) Since A and B are disjoint P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)=0.85.

Example 10. Let A,B and C be the events X is a multiple of 2, 3 and 6 respectively. If P(A)=0.6, P(B)=0.3 and P(C)=0.2 what is P(A or B)?

Solution: Note two things. First we used the word ‘or’ which means union: ‘A or B’ = A∪B. Second, an integer is divisible by 6 if and only if it is divisible by both 2 and 3.

This translates into C=A∩B. So the inclusion-exclusion principle says

P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)=0.6+0.3−0.2=0.7.

例 8 用补集得奇数概率 1−0.6=0.4. 例 9 中“偶数”和“奇数且小于 10”不可能同时发生, 所以交集为空, 并集概率 0.6+0.25=0.85.

例 10 的关键是“能被 6 整除”等价于“同时能被 2 和 3 整除”, 因而 C=A∩B. 用容斥得 P(A∪B)=0.6+0.3−0.2=0.7. 这里不需要假设 1 到 20 等可能, 因为使用的是事件概率而不是按个数计算.

Concept Question

Lucky Lucy has a coin that you’re quite sure is not fair.

  • They will flip the coin twice
  • It’s your job to say whether it more probable that the tosses are the same, i.e. HH or TT, or different, i.e. HT or TH.

Which should you choose?

  1. More probable they are the same
  2. More probable they are different
  3. Doesn’t matter
  4. It depends on the actual probabilities of getting heads or tails.

在通常的独立抛掷假设下, 应选 (1). 设正面概率为 p, 则两次相同的概率为 p2+(1−p)2, 不同的概率为 2p(1−p). 下一页的代数推导表明, 非公平硬币前者严格更大.

原文笔误:whether it more probable 缺少系动词, 应为 whether it is more probable.

Slide 20

Board Question

Lucky Lucy has a coin that you’re quite sure is not fair.

  • They will flip the coin twice
  • Let A be the event the tosses are the same, i.e. {HH,TT}
  • Let B be the event the tosses are the different, i.e. {HT,TH}

Let p be the probability of heads. Compute and compare P(A) and P(B).

(If you don’t see the symbolic algebra try p=0.2, p=0.5)

P(A)=p2+(1−p)2, P(B)=2p(1−p). 两式相减:

P(A)−P(B)=p2+(1−p)2−2p(1−p)=(2p−1)2≥0.

若硬币非公平, p=1/2, 则 P(A)>P(B); 若 p=1/2, 两者都等于 1/2. 例如 p=0.2 时, P(A)=0.68、P(B)=0.32.

易错:这个结论使用两次抛掷独立且正面概率不变. 若允许两次结果相关, 仅知道“硬币非公平”并不足以比较两个事件.

原文笔误:the tosses are the different 多了 the, 应为 the tosses are different.
