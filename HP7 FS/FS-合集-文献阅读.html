Weizenbaum: ELIZA (1966)
ELIZA — A Computer Program For the Study of Natural Language Communication Between Man And Machine

这是 ELIZA 的原始论文, 发表在 1966 年 1 月的 Communications of the ACM 「Computational Linguistics」栏目. 扫描件是期刊原页. 全文三部分: 程序的工作原理 (S. 36–42), 讨论 (S. 42–43), 附录里一份完整的 ELIZA 脚本 (S. 44–45). 练习只要求抓主线: 程序怎样用「关键词 + 变换规则」产生回答, 以及作者自己对「理解」的反思.

摘要与引言: 解释即祛魅
Abstract · Introduction

S. 36

ELIZA is a program operating within the MAC time-sharing system at MIT which makes certain kinds of natural language conversation between man and computer possible. Input sentences are analyzed on the basis of decomposition rules which are triggered by key words appearing in the input text. Responses are generated by reassembly rules associated with selected decomposition rules. The fundamental technical problems with which ELIZA is concerned are: (1) the identification of key words, (2) the discovery of minimal context, (3) the choice of appropriate transformations, (4) generation of responses in the absence of key words, and (5) the provision of an editing capability for ELIZA “scripts”. A discussion of some psychological issues relevant to the ELIZA approach as well as of future developments concludes the paper.

It is said that to explain is to explain away. This maxim is nowhere so well fulfilled as in the area of computer programming, especially in what is called heuristic programming and artificial intelligence. For in those realms machines are made to behave in wondrous ways, often sufficient to dazzle even the most experienced observer. But once a particular program is unmasked, once its inner workings are explained in language sufficiently plain to induce understanding, its magic crumbles away; it stands revealed as a mere collection of procedures, each quite comprehensible. The observer says to himself “I could have written that”. With that thought he moves the program in question from the shelf marked “intelligent”, to that reserved for curios, fit to be discussed only with people less enlightened than he.

The object of this paper is to cause just such a re-evaluation of the program about to be “explained”. Few programs ever needed it more.

摘要: ELIZA 运行在 MIT 的 MAC 分时系统上, 让人和计算机之间能进行某些类型的自然语言对话. 输入句按Zerlegungsregeln 分解规则 (decomposition rules) 分析, 分解规则由输入中出现的Schlüsselwörter 关键词触发; 回答由与分解规则关联的重组规则 (reassembly rules) 生成. 五个技术问题: ① 识别关键词; ② 找出最小语境; ③ 选择合适的变换; ④ 没有关键词时也能回答; ⑤ 提供编辑 ELIZA「脚本」的功能. 最后讨论心理学问题和未来发展.

引言的意思: 俗话说「解释就是把它解释掉」. 在启发式编程和人工智能领域尤其如此: 机器表现得令人惊叹, 可一旦把内部机制用浅白的语言讲清楚, 魔力就消失了, 程序只是一堆各自都很好懂的过程, 观察者会想「这我也写得出来」, 把它从「智能」的架子挪到「古董」的架子上. 本文的目的就是让读者对 ELIZA 做这样的重新评价, 「很少有程序比它更需要这样做」.

这句开场白定下了全文的基调: 作者不是在炫耀一个智能程序, 而是在拆穿它.

程序与一段典型对话
ELIZA Program

S. 36–37

ELIZA is a program which makes natural language conversation with a computer possible. Its present implementation is on the MAC time-sharing system at MIT. It is written in MAD-SLIP [4] for the IBM 7094. Its name was chosen to emphasize that it may be incrementally improved by its users, since its language abilities may be continually improved by a “teacher”. Like the Eliza of Pygmalion fame, it can be made to appear even more civilized, the relation of appearance to reality, however, remaining in the domain of the playwright.

… When in conversation with ELIZA, the user types in some statement or set of statements in natural language using normal punctuation and sentence structures. Only the question mark may not be used, because it is interpreted as a line delete character by the MAC system. …

A typical conversation is the following:

Men are all alike.
IN WHAT WAY
They're always bugging us about something or other.
CAN YOU THINK OF A SPECIFIC EXAMPLE
Well, my boyfriend made me come here.
YOUR BOYFRIEND MADE YOU COME HERE
He says I'm depressed much of the time.
I AM SORRY TO HEAR YOU ARE DEPRESSED
It's true. I am unhappy.
DO YOU THINK COMING HERE WILL HELP YOU NOT TO BE UNHAPPY
I need some help, that much seems certain.
WHAT WOULD IT MEAN TO YOU IF YOU GOT SOME HELP
Perhaps I could learn to get along with my mother.
TELL ME MORE ABOUT YOUR FAMILY
My mother takes care of me.
WHO ELSE IN YOUR FAMILY TAKES CARE OF YOU
My father.
YOUR FATHER
You are like my father in some ways.
WHAT RESEMBLANCE DO YOU SEE
…
Bullies.
DOES THAT HAVE ANYTHING TO DO WITH THE FACT THAT YOUR BOYFRIEND MADE YOU COME HERE

The capitalized lines are the machine responses.

ELIZA 用 MAD-SLIP (Weizenbaum 自己设计的表处理语言 SLIP 嵌在 MAD 语言里) 写成, 运行在 IBM 7094 上. 名字来自萧伯纳的剧本《卖花女》(Pygmalion) 里的 Eliza Doolittle: 她被语言学教授教会了上流社会的说话方式, 程序也可以被「老师」(写脚本的人) 一点点教得更「体面」; 但「表象和现实的关系, 仍然属于剧作家的领域」, 即外表像不等于真的是. 用户在远程打字机上输入, 正常使用标点, 但不能用问号 (MAC 系统把它当作删除整行的控制符), 这就是对话里用户的问句也没有问号的原因.

大写行是机器的回答. 这段对话里能看到 ELIZA 的全部套路: 把 my 换成 your (YOUR BOYFRIEND MADE YOU COME HERE); 按关键词给固定反应 (mother → TELL ME MORE ABOUT YOUR FAMILY); 没有关键词时说空话 (IN WHAT WAY). 最后一句最让人惊讶: 它把前面说过的 boyfriend 拿了回来, 这靠的是后面讲的 MEMORY 机制.

关键词、分解规则、重组规则
Keywords, decomposition and reassembly

S. 37

The gross procedure of the program is quite simple; the text is read and inspected for the presence of a keyword. If such a word is found, the sentence is transformed according to a rule associated with the keyword, if not a content-free remark or, under certain conditions, an earlier transformation is retrieved. The text so computed or retrieved is then printed out.

In detail, of course, the procedure sketched above is considerably more complex. Keywords, for example, may have a RANK or precedence number. … Also, the procedure recognizes a comma or a period as a delimiter. Whenever either one is encountered and a keyword has already been found, all subsequent text is deleted from the input message. If no key had yet been found the phrase or sentence to the left of the delimiter (as well as the delimiter itself) is deleted. As a result, only single phrases or sentences are ever transformed.

Keywords and their associated transformation¹ rules constitute the SCRIPT for a particular class of conversation. An important property of ELIZA is that a script is data; i.e., it is not part of the program itself. Hence, ELIZA is not restricted to a particular set of recognition patterns or responses, indeed not even to any specific language. ELIZA scripts exist (at this writing) in Welsh and German as well as in English.

¹ The word “transformation” is used in its generic sense rather than that given it by Harris and Chomsky in linguistic contexts.

S. 37–38

Consider the sentence “I am very unhappy these days”. Suppose a foreigner with only a limited knowledge of English but with a very good ear heard that sentence spoken but understood only the first two words “I am”. Wishing to appear interested, perhaps even sympathetic, he may reply “How long have you been very unhappy these days?” What he must have done is to apply a kind of template to the original sentence, one part of which matched the two words “I am” and the remainder isolated the words “very unhappy these days”. … A somewhat more complicated example is given by the sentence “It seems that you hate me”. Here the foreigner understands only the words “you” and “me”; i.e., he applies a template that decomposes the sentence into the four parts:

(1) It seems that   (2) you   (3) hate   (4) me

of which only the second and fourth parts are understood. The reassembly rule might then be “What makes you think I hate you”; …

A formal notation in which to represent the decomposition template is:

(0 YOU 0 ME)

and the reassembly rule

(WHAT MAKES YOU THINK I 3 YOU).

The “0” in the decomposition rule stands for “an indefinite number of words” (analogous to the indefinite dollar sign of COMIT) [6] while the “3” in the reassembly rule indicates that the third component of the subject decomposition is to be inserted in its place. The decomposition rule

(0 YOU 1 ME)

would have worked just as well in this specific example. A nonzero integer “n” appearing in a decomposition rule indicates that the component in question should consist of exactly “n” words. However, of the two rules shown, only the first would have matched the sentence, “It seems you hate and love me,” the second failing because there is more than one word between “you” and “me”.

总流程: 读入文本, 找关键词; 找到就按该关键词的规则变换句子; 找不到就说一句不含内容的话, 或在某些条件下取出一个之前存下的变换结果; 打印出来. 细节: 关键词可以有等级 (RANK); 逗号和句号是分隔符: 已经找到关键词时, 分隔符之后的内容全部丢掉; 还没找到时, 丢掉分隔符左边的部分. 结果是每次只变换一个短语或句子.

脚本 (script) = 关键词 + 它们的变换规则. 关键性质: 脚本是数据, 不是程序的一部分, 所以 ELIZA 不限于某一套模式和回答, 甚至不限于某种语言 (当时已有威尔士语和德语脚本). 脚注说明这里的 transformation 是一般意义的「变换」, 不是 Harris 和 Chomsky 语言学里的「转换」.

外国人的比喻: 一个英语很差但耳朵很好的外国人, 只听懂了「I am」, 就套一个模板, 把剩下的部分原样放进回答: 「I am very unhappy these days」→「How long have you been very unhappy these days?」. ELIZA 做的就是这件事.

形式记法: 分解规则 (0 YOU 0 ME) 把句子切成四段, 0 表示「任意多个词」(包括零个), YOU、ME 是要逐字匹配的词. 重组规则 (WHAT MAKES YOU THINK I 3 YOU) 里的数字 3 表示「放入分解结果的第 3 段」. 分解规则里的非零整数 n 表示「恰好 n 个词」: (0 YOU 1 ME) 也能匹配「It seems that you hate me」, 但匹配不了「It seems you hate and love me」, 因为 you 和 me 之间有 3 个词.

ELIZA 记法正则表达式 (eliza.py 的写法)含义
0(.*)任意多个词
1(\S+)恰好一个词
YOUyou逐字匹配的词
重组里的 3回答里的 %1 等插入第几段 (Python 版只给括号编号)

COMIT 是 Victor Yngve 在 MIT 设计的字符串处理语言 (参考文献 [6]), 专门为机器翻译做模式匹配, 它的 $ 表示任意长度的串. ELIZA 的 0 就借用了这个想法. 今天的正则表达式 .* 是同一个概念.

关键词表的结构与查找
Key list structure

S. 38

In ELIZA the question of which decomposition rules to apply to an input text is of course a crucial one. … ELIZA must therefore have a mechanism to sharply delimit the set of decomposition rules which are potentially applicable to a currently active input sentence. This is the keyword mechanism.

An input sentence is scanned from left to right. Each word is looked up in a dictionary of keywords. If a word is identified as a keyword, then (apart from the issue of precedence of keywords) only decomposition rules containing that keyword need to be tried. The trial sequence can even be partially ordered. For example, the decomposition rule (0 YOU 0) associated with the keyword “YOU” … should be the last one tried since it is bound to succeed.

… The attack on both problems begins by placing both a keyword and its associated rules on a list. The basic format of a typical key list is the following:

(K ((D1) (R1,1) (R1,2) ··· (R1,m1))
   ((D2) (R2,1) (R2,2) ··· (R2,m2))
       ⋮
   ((Dn) (Rn,1) (Rn,2) ··· (Rn,mn)))

where K is the keyword, Di the ith decomposition rule associated with K and Ri,j the jth reassembly rule associated with the ith decomposition rule.

… An ELIZA script consists mainly of a set of list structures of the type shown. The actual keyword dictionary is constructed when such a script is first read into the hitherto empty program. The basic structural component of the keyword dictionary is a vector KEY of (currently) 128 contiguous computer words. As a particular key list structure is read the keyword K at its top is randomized (hashed) by a procedure that produces (currently) a 7 bit integer “i”. … The largest dictionary so far attempted contains about 50 keywords.

哪条分解规则适用于当前输入, 是核心问题; 关键词机制就是用来缩小候选规则范围的. 输入从左到右扫描, 每个词在关键词词典里查; 是关键词就只试这个关键词下的分解规则. 规则可以有先后: 像 (0 YOU 0) 这种一定成功的规则应该最后试.

关键词表: 一个关键词 K, 下面挂 n 条分解规则 Di, 每条分解规则又挂 mi 条重组规则 Ri,j. 读图: 树根是 K, 第二层每个节点是一组「Di 和它的重组规则」, 数量都不受限制.

查找用散列 (hashing): 关键词被散列成 7 位整数 i (0–127), 放进长 128 的向量 KEY 的第 i 格; 冲突时挂在同一格的列表底部. 扫描输入时每个词用同一个散列函数算出位置, 只需查那一格. 当时最大的词典约 50 个关键词.

对比练习里的 eliza.py: 它没有关键词词典, 而是把所有正则规则排成一列, 每次从头逐条试.

简单替换、关键词等级与关键词栈
Substitutions · Ranks · Keystack

S. 39

Some conversational protocols require that certain transformations be made on certain words of the input text independently of any contextual considerations. The first conversation displayed in this paper, for example, requires that first person pronouns be exchanged for second person pronouns and vice versa throughout the input text. … any word in the key dictionary, i.e., at the top of a key list structure, may be followed by an equal sign followed by whatever word is to be its substitute. Tranformation rules may, but need not, follow. … An example of a simple substitution is

(YOURSELF = MYSELF).

… Suppose an input sentence is “I know everybody laughed at me.” A script may tag the word “I” as well as the word “everybody” as a keyword. Without differential ranking, “I” occurring first would determine the transformation to be applied. A typical response might be “You say you know everybody laughed at you.” But the important message in the input sentence begins with the word “everybody”. It is very often true that when a person speaks in terms of universals such as “everybody”, “always” and “nobody” he is really referring to some quite specific event or person. By giving “everybody” a higher rank than “I”, the response “Who in particular are you thinking of” may be generated.

The specific mechanism employed in ranking is that the rank of every keyword encountered (absence of rank implies rank equals 0) is compared with the rank of the highest ranked keyword already seen. If the rank of the new word is higher than that of any previously encountered word, the pointer to the transformation rules associated with the new word is placed on top of a list called the keystack, otherwise it is placed on the bottom of the keystack. …

… As an example consider the word “MY” in a particular script. Its keyword list may be as follows:

(MY = YOUR 5 (transformation rules)).

Such a list would mean that whenever the word “MY” is encountered in any text, it would be replaced by the word “YOUR”. Its rank would be 5.

简单替换: 有些变换与语境无关, 例如对话里要把第一人称和第二人称代词全部互换. 这不必写成完整的变换规则: 在关键词词典里写 (YOURSELF = MYSELF), 扫描时就地替换. 一个词可以只做替换, 也可以既替换又是关键词. 这就是 eliza.py 里 reflections 字典的来源.

等级: 「I know everybody laughed at me」里 I 和 everybody 都是关键词. 不分等级时先出现的 I 决定回答 (「You say you know everybody laughed at you」); 但重要的信息在 everybody: 人说「所有人」「总是」「没人」时, 往往其实指某件具体的事或某个具体的人. 给 everybody 更高的等级, 就能回答「Who in particular are you thinking of」. 机制: 没写等级的关键词等级为 0; 新关键词的等级高于已见过的所有关键词, 就把它的规则指针放在关键词栈 (keystack) 顶部, 否则放在底部. 扫描结束时栈顶就是等级最高的关键词. (MY = YOUR 5 …) 表示 MY 替换成 YOUR, 等级 5.

读图: 从 BEGIN 开始清空栈、P (当前最高等级) 置 0, 读文本; 逐词取 W: 文本结束就到 END; W 是逗号或句号时, 栈空就删掉 W 和它前面的词 (还没找到关键词, 丢掉前一个分句), 栈不空就删掉 W 和它后面的所有词 (已有关键词, 后面的不要了); W 是关键词时比较它的等级和 P, 更高就放栈顶并更新 P, 否则放栈底.

原文笔误: S. 39 左栏「Tranformation rules may, but need not, follow」少了一个 s, 应为 Transformation.

按顺序轮换重组规则
Cycling through reassembly rules

S. 40

Consider again the input text

You are very helpful

in which “you” is the only key word. The sentence is transformed during scanning to

I are very helpful

{(D1), you} is “(0 I remind your of 0)” and fails to match as already discussed. However, {(D2), you} is “(0 I are 0)” and obviously matches the text, decomposing it into the constituents

(1) empty   (2) I   (3) are   (4) very helpful.

{(R2,1), you} is

(What makes you think I am 4)

Hence it produces the output text

What makes you think I am very helpful.

Having produced it, the integer 1 is put in front of (R2,1) … Next time {(D2), K} matches an input text, the reassembly rule (R2,2) will be applied … This mechanism insures that the complete set of reassembly rules associated with a given decomposition rule is cycled through before any repetitions occur.

完整例子: 「You are very helpful」里唯一的关键词是 you; 扫描时 you 已被替换成 I, 得到「I are very helpful」. 第一条分解规则 (0 I remind you of 0) 匹配失败, 第二条 (0 I are 0) 成功, 切成四段 (第 1 段为空). 第一条重组规则「What makes you think I am 4」把第 4 段放进去, 输出「What makes you think I am very helpful」. 用过的重组规则会被记下, 下次同一条分解规则匹配时用下一条重组规则, 全部轮完一遍才会重复.

对比: eliza.py 用 random.choice 随机挑回答, 所以可能连续两次说同一句; 原版是轮换, 不会过早重复.

原文前后不一致: S. 40 第一行把 {(D1), you} 写成「(0 I remind you of 0)」, 几行后同一条规则又写成「(0 I remind your of 0)」; 应为 you.

等价类、PRE、NEWKEY、NONE、MEMORY 与标签
(=WHAT) · PRE · NEWKEY · NONE · MEMORY · DLIST

S. 40–42

… For example, the keyword “what” may have associated with it a transformation rule set of the form

((0) (Why do you ask) (Is that an important question) . . .)

which would apply equally well to the keywords “how” and “when”. The entire keyword list for “how” may therefore be

(How (=What))

… The dictionary entry for “you’re” is therefore:

(you’re = I’m ((0 I’m 0) (PRE (I AM 3) (=YOU))))

… Another form of reassembly rule is (NEWKEY) which serves the case in which attempts to match on the currently regnant keyword are to be given up and the entire decomposition and reassembly process is to start again on the basis of the keyword to be found in the keystack. …

A serious problem which remains to be discussed is the reaction of the system in case no keywords remain to serve as transformation triggers. … The simplest mechanism supplied is in the form of the special reserved keyword “NONE” which must be part of any script. The script writer must associate the universally matching decomposition rule (0) with it and follow this by as many content-free remarks in the form of transformation rules as he pleases. (Examples are: “Please go on”, “That’s very interesting” and “I see”.)

There is, however, another mechanism which causes the system to respond more spectacularly in the absence of a key. The word “MEMORY” is another reserved pseudo-keyword. …

(MEMORY MY
   (0 YOUR 0 = LETS DISCUSS FURTHER WHY YOUR 3)
   (0 YOUR 0 = EARLIER YOU SAID YOUR 3)
   ⋮

The word “MY” … Whenever it is the highest ranking keyword of a text one of the transformations on the MEMORY list is randomly selected, and a copy of the text is transformed accordingly. This transformation is stored on a first-in-first-out stack for later use. … When a text without keywords is encountered later and a certain counting mechanism is in a particular state and the stack in question is not empty, then the transformed text is printed out as the reply.

… The keyword “MOTHER” in ELIZA, for example, may be identified as a noun and as a member of the class “family” as follows:

(MOTHER DLIST (/NOUN FAMILY)).

… The above input text would have been decomposed precisely as stated above by the decomposition rule:

(0 YOUR 0 (*FATHER MOTHER) 0)
  • 等价类 (=WHAT): how、when 和 what 共用一套规则, 不必各存一份.
  • 预变换 PRE: you’re 先替换成 I’m, 按 (0 I’m 0) 切分, 再用 (PRE (I AM 3) (=YOU)) 重写成「I AM + 第 3 段」, 然后交给关键词 you 的规则继续处理.
  • NEWKEY: 当前关键词的规则都不合适时, 弹出关键词栈顶, 换下一个关键词从头再来. 这样就能处理关键短语, 而不只是单个关键词.
  • NONE: 没有关键词时的保底, 配上万能分解规则 (0) 和一串不含内容的话 (Please go on、I see …). 对应 eliza.py 的最后一条 (.*).
  • MEMORY: 当 MY 是输入中等级最高的关键词时, 随机选一条 MEMORY 变换, 把这句话改写后存进先进先出的栈; 以后遇到没有关键词的输入 (并且计数器处于某个状态), 就把存下的话拿出来说. 开头对话最后那句「DOES THAT HAVE ANYTHING TO DO WITH THE FACT THAT YOUR BOYFRIEND MADE YOU COME HERE」就是这样来的, 这也是 ELIZA 显得「记得」的唯一原因.
  • 标签 DLIST: 关键词可以带标签, 如 MOTHER 标为名词和 FAMILY 类; 分解规则里写 (/FAMILY) 就匹配任何带此标签的词. (*FATHER MOTHER) 表示「FATHER 或 MOTHER」(选择).

此外脚本以一句开场白开始; 输入 EDIT 时 ELIZA 进入休眠, 调用 MAC 的上下文编辑程序 ED 修改脚本, 改完 FILE 存盘, 再打印 START 继续对话. 作者认为脚本应该从一小套关键词开始, 在使用中逐步扩充.

讨论: 罗杰斯派治疗师、可信度与理解
Discussion

S. 42

At this writing, the only serious ELIZA scripts which exist are some which cause ELIZA to respond roughly as would certain psychotherapists (Rogerians). ELIZA performs best when its human correspondent is initially instructed to “talk” to it, via the typewriter of course, just as one would to a psychiatrist. This mode of conversation was chosen because the psychiatric interview is one of the few examples of categorized dyadic natural language communication in which one of the participating pair is free to assume the pose of knowing almost nothing of the real world. If, for example, one were to tell a psychiatrist “I went for a long boat ride” and he responded “Tell me about boats”, one would not assume that he knew nothing about boats, but that he had some purpose in so directing the subsequent conversation.

… With ELIZA as the basic vehicle, experiments may be set up in which the subjects find it credible to believe that the responses which appear on his typewriter are generated by a human sitting at a similar instrument in another room. … Some subjects have been very hard to convince that ELIZA (with its present script) is not human. This is a striking form of Turing’s test. …

… ELIZA shows, if nothing else, how easy it is to create and maintain the illusion of understanding, hence perhaps of judgment deserving of credibility. A certain danger lurks there.

S. 43

… The crucial test of understanding, as every teacher should know, is not the subject’s ability to continue a conversation, but to draw valid conclusions from what he is being told. In order for a computer program to be able to do that, it must at least have the capacity to store selected parts of its inputs. ELIZA throws away each of its inputs, except for those few transformed by means of the MEMORY machinery. …

… In the long run, ELIZA should be able to build up a belief structure (to use Abelson’s phrase) of the subject and on that basis detect the subject’s rationalizations, contradictions, etc. …

… Seen in the coldest possible light, ELIZA is a translating processor in Gorn’s sense; however, it is one which has been especially constructed to work well with natural language text.

为什么选心理治疗师: 精神科面谈是少数几种「对话中一方可以装作对现实世界几乎一无所知」的双人对话. 你对精神科医生说「我去划了很久的船」, 他回「说说船吧」, 你不会认为他不懂船, 而会认为他有意引导谈话. 这个假设是说话人做出的; 它让 ELIZA 的脚本不必存储任何关于现实世界的知识.

理解的错觉: 说话人会主动给 ELIZA 的回答「穿上可信的外衣」, 把各种背景知识、洞察力和推理能力归给对方; 难以解释的回答反而可能被说话人合理化, 让对方的形象更高明. 有些受试者很难被说服 ELIZA 不是人, 作者称之为「图灵测试的一种惊人形式」. 结论: ELIZA 至少说明, 制造和维持「被理解」(以及「值得信任的判断」) 的错觉有多容易, 这里潜藏着危险.

真正的理解: 理解的关键检验不是能不能把对话继续下去, 而是能不能从听到的话里得出有效的结论. 为此程序至少要能保存输入中被选出的部分; ELIZA 除了 MEMORY 之外把输入全扔了. 作者设想更好的 ELIZA 应该能逐步建立说话人的信念结构 (belief structure), 发现对方的自我辩解和矛盾 (例如对方先说没结婚、后来又提到妻子). 最后他用 Gorn 的说法给 ELIZA 定性: 冷静地看, 它只是一个翻译处理器 (把一种语言的表达式按句法规则变成另一种语言的表达式), 只不过特别适合处理自然语言文本.
